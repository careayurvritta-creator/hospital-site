Ayurveda's external care is one of its most tangible gifts. Long before modern dermatology, physicians prepared medicated oils and herbal pastes for skin disease, and these remedies remain central to treatment today. Together, oils and **Lepas** offer targeted comfort while internal care addresses the roots of the problem.

## External Care in the Ayurvedic Toolkit

Skin disorders grouped under **Kushta** are treated from two directions at once. Internal medicine works on the doshas, **Agni** (digestive fire), and **Ama** (metabolic toxins), while external applications work directly on the lesion. The classics describe this pairing repeatedly, and patients generally feel the difference it makes. External care eases itching, softens scaling, cools burning, and supports the skin barrier while the slower internal work proceeds. It is also the part of treatment families can see and feel, which helps confidence during a long condition.

## Medicated Oils: More Than Moisture

Ayurvedic medicated oils, known as Taila, are not simple carrier oils with fragrance. They are prepared by cooking selected herbs into a base oil or ghee over low heat for many hours, a process that allows the oil-soluble properties of the herbs to be absorbed into the medium.

- **Classical preparation:** the method of cooking, the herb selection, and the base all follow text-based protocols rather than quick mixing.
- **Dual action:** the oil base nourishes and protects the skin while the medicated herbs work on local dosha disturbance.
- **Choice matters:** cooling oils tend to suit Pitta-type redness and burning, while warming, lighter oils often suit Vata-type dryness and cracking.
- **Everyday support:** plain warm coconut or sesame oil is often recommended between treatments to keep the skin supple.
- **Prescription-based:** therapeutic oils are selected by the physician; a product that suits one pattern can aggravate another.

## Lepas: Herbal Pastes Applied to the Skin

A Lepa is a paste of herbs, usually freshly prepared, applied in a thin, even layer over the affected area and removed after a set time. The word itself carries the sense of smearing or anointing, and the therapy appears throughout classical dermatology.

- **Freshly made:** Lepas are typically mixed shortly before application so the herbs remain active.
- **Even layers:** the paste is spread gently over the lesion, avoiding broken or heavily infected skin.
- **Timed contact:** it is kept for a defined period and then washed off with lukewarm water or a mild herbal decoction.
- **Matched to the condition:** cooling pastes suit inflamed, burning patches, while drying, lightening pastes suit oozing or thickened lesions.
- **Gentle removal:** scrubbing is never advised; the paste is softened with water and eased away.

Application frequency and duration are decided by the physician, since over-application can irritate sensitive or broken skin.

## Simple Supportive Care at Home

Between visits, gentle home habits can carry the treatment forward.

- **Moisturize simply:** plain coconut oil or a physician-approved base keeps dry, scaling skin comfortable.
- **Lukewarm bathing:** short baths with mild cleansers protect the skin's natural barrier.
- **Patch testing:** even natural pastes deserve a small test on healthy skin before wider use, since allergies to plants do occur.
- **Keep it clean:** separate towels, washed clothing, and dry skin folds prevent secondary infection.
- **Avoid irritants:** strong soaps, deodorants, and heavily perfumed lotions are best set aside during active flares.

## Making External Care Work Harder

External applications succeed best inside a wider plan. Following **Pathya-Apathya** dietary guidance, keeping meals warm and regular to support Agni, managing stress, and honoring seasonal routines all reduce the burden on the skin. When flares are active, physicians may pair external care with **Shodhana** (purification) procedures at the right time, then move to **Rasayana** (rejuvenation) support once the skin settles. External care is the visible thread of a treatment whose deeper work happens inside.

## Who Should Use Care

External does not mean risk-free. Do not apply medicated oils or Lepas to deep wounds, actively infected skin, or the eyes and genitals without explicit medical instruction. Children, pregnant or nursing women, and people with known plant allergies should use preparations only under supervision. If an application causes burning, swelling, or spreading redness, wash it off and contact your physician. Never use topical preparations sold without ingredient lists, and never start a medicated oil alongside a new prescription cream without informing both practitioners.

Medicated oils and Lepas bring classical Ayurveda's healing directly to the skin, easing symptoms while the root pattern is addressed within.
