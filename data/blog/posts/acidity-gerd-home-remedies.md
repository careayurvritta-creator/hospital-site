Acidity is one of the most common complaints we hear — and one of the most misunderstood. In Ayurveda, the condition is called **Amlapitta**: *amla* (sour) and *pitta* (the fire/transformation principle). The name captures the mechanism precisely — an excess of sour, hot, and sharp qualities in the stomach that refluxes upward as burning, belching, and sour taste.

## Why the Burn Happens

Pitta is the digestive fire. In health, it digests food calmly. When aggravated, it becomes like an over-hot stove — producing excess acid, irritation, and a burning sensation. Common aggravators include:

1. Spicy, fried, and fermented foods eaten late at night.
2. Irregular eating — long gaps followed by heavy meals.
3. Suppressing hunger.
4. Chronic stress, anger, and a rushed lifestyle.
5. Excessive tea, coffee, alcohol, and smoking.

## Home Remedies That Settle the Fire

### Amla (Indian Gooseberry)

A tablespoon of fresh amla juice or amla powder with warm water, in the morning, is one of the best natural cooling agents. Amla is a natural *Pitta pacifier* and is the first thing many physicians recommend.

### Shatavari and Licorice (Yashtimadhu)

- **Licorice** (mulethi) — a small piece chewed after meals soothes the stomach lining. Use it sparingly; prolonged high-dose use is not advised, especially with high blood pressure.
- **Shatavari** — especially helpful for women with acid reflux linked to hormonal cycles.

### Cold Milk with Ghee

A glass of cold milk with a teaspoon of ghee before bed coats and cools the stomach lining. This is a time-honoured *Pitta pacifying* evening ritual.

### Cooling Herbal Teas

- Cumin, coriander, and fennel seeds, gently boiled — sip warm after meals.
- Fresh coriander leaves blended into a light drink.
- Rose water or a few rose petals infused in water.

### Triphala at Night

A teaspoon of Triphala with warm water at bedtime improves digestion and gently detoxifies — regularity is closely tied to fewer acid flare-ups.

## Foods That Pacify Pitta

### Prefer

- Sweet, ripe fruits — banana, melon, pomegranate, and sweet apple.
- Cucumber, bottle gourd, and zucchini.
- Ghee, milk, and fresh yogurt (not sour).
- Basmati rice with mild moong dal.
- Coconut and tender coconut water.

### Avoid

- Red chilies, excessive garlic and onion, and pickles.
- Fermented foods, vinegar, and citrus in excess.
- Tea, coffee, alcohol, and carbonated drinks.
- Deep-fried and reheated food.
- Eating late at night or lying down right after meals.

## Daily Habits That Prevent Recurrence

1. Eat your largest meal at noon when Agni is strongest.
2. Keep dinner light and at least three hours before sleep.
3. Take a gentle 10-minute walk after meals.
4. Drink warm water through the day; avoid ice-cold water.
5. Manage stress — even five minutes of slow, deep breathing before meals helps.

## When Virechana Is Considered

When acidity is chronic despite good diet and habits, a physician may recommend **Virechana** (therapeutic purgation) — the classical *Pitta* detox. It clears excess Pitta and Ama from the system and is followed by a specific recovery diet. This is always done under medical supervision, after preparation and assessment.

## When to See a Doctor

If you have difficulty swallowing, unexplained weight loss, blood in the vomit or stool, or symptoms that persist despite three to four weeks of care, please see a physician promptly — Ayurvedic care complements, but never replaces, the evaluation needed for serious conditions.
