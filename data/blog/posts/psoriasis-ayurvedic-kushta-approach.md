Psoriasis is often described as a skin disease, yet anyone living with it knows the problem runs deeper than the surface. Thick, scaly patches arrive, itch relentlessly, and return just when things seemed settled. Classical Ayurveda places this condition within **Kushta**, the ancient category for persistent skin disorders, and reads it as a whole-body pattern rather than a purely local one.

## What Kushta Tells Us About Psoriasis

The term Kushta appears throughout the Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita as an umbrella for skin disorders in which the doshas are deeply involved. These texts do not treat Kushta as a cosmetic annoyance. They describe it as a condition where disturbed doshas, weakened digestion, and contaminated tissues converge and express themselves through the skin. That framing matters, because it explains why topical care alone rarely settles psoriasis for long. Ayurveda looks at the terrain beneath the patch.

## The Doshas Behind the Patches

The three doshas are the functional energies of the body: **Vata** governs movement and dryness, **Pitta** governs transformation and heat, and **Kapha** governs structure and moisture. In psoriasis, each leaves a distinct mark.

- **Vata influence:** dryness, rough scaling, painful cracking, and itching that worsens in cold or windy weather.
- **Pitta influence:** redness, heat, burning sensations, and flares that follow spicy food, alcohol, or strong sun.
- **Kapha influence:** thickened skin, heaviness, sticky discharge, and slow-healing lesions.

Most patients show a blend, which is why two people with the same diagnosis may receive quite different plans. A qualified Ayurvedic physician studies which doshas dominate before selecting herbs or therapies.

## Agni, Ama, and the Deeper Tissues

Central to the Ayurvedic reading of Kushta is **Agni**, the digestive and metabolic fire that transforms food into usable nutrition. When Agni is irregular or weak, incomplete digestion produces **Ama**, a sticky metabolic residue the body cannot easily clear. Classical texts describe Ama travelling through the body's channels and lodging in the **Dhatus**, the seven body tissues. In skin disorders, the Rasa tissue (nutrient plasma) and Rakta tissue (blood) are most often affected, which is why the skin becomes the visible outlet of an internal imbalance. Supporting Agni and clearing Ama matter just as much as soothing the skin itself.

## A Physician-Guided Care Approach

Ayurvedic care for psoriasis is staged rather than one-size-fits-all.

1. **Assessment:** the physician studies dosha patterns, digestion, sleep, stress, and the history of flares.
2. **Deepana and Pachana:** gentle measures to rekindle Agni and digest Ama, often beginning with simple diet adjustments.
3. **Shodhana:** purification therapies, planned according to strength, season, and severity, to clear accumulated doshas.
4. **Shamana:** calming internal and external care, including medicated oils and herbal pastes suited to the individual's skin.
5. **Rasayana:** rejuvenation support aimed at building resilience and lengthening the gap between flares.

Each stage is tailored. Timing and intensity depend on age, strength, and how active the condition is.

## Diet and Daily Habits That Support the Skin

Ayurveda pairs treatment with **Pathya-Apathya**, the classical understanding of favorable and unfavorable foods and habits. Many patients with Kushta find the following supportive.

- **Warm, freshly cooked meals:** light soups, khichdi, and steamed vegetables are gentler on a sluggish Agni.
- **Bitter and astringent tastes:** foods such as bitter gourd, fenugreek, and turmeric are traditionally favored in skin disorders.
- **Reduced irritants:** pickles, curd at night, deep-fried snacks, alcohol, and very spicy food commonly worsen flares.
- **Steady routine:** regular sleep, moderate exercise, and stress management help keep Vata and Pitta calm.
- **Gentle skin habits:** lukewarm baths instead of hot ones, mild cleansers, and simple moisturizers reduce daily irritation.

## Who Should Use Care

Ayurvedic approaches work best alongside sensible medical oversight. If patches bleed, crack deeply, cover large areas of the body, or appear with joint pain or fever, please see a doctor promptly. Pregnant or nursing women, children, and elderly or frail patients should follow herbal and purification protocols only under direct supervision. Never stop prescribed medication without consulting the prescribing doctor, and share your full medical history so herbs and procedures are chosen safely. Some patients respond slowly, and realistic expectations help everyone.
