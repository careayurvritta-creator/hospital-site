Few foods have had as dramatic a reversal in reputation as ghee. For decades, it was the villain of the Indian diet — blamed for heart disease and cholesterol. Today, a more careful picture has emerged, and it aligns remarkably with what Ayurveda has said for thousands of years: **ghee, used correctly, is one of the most valuable foods in the diet.** The word for it in Sanskrit is *Ghrita* — and Ayurveda calls it *amritam* (nectar), a carrier of medicine and a nourisher of the deepest tissues.

## What Ayurveda Says About Ghee

- **Ojas** — ghee is considered the finest builder of Ojas (vitality and immunity).
- **Agni** — it kindles the digestive fire without increasing heat or inflammation (unlike many oils).
- **Rasayana** — a rejuvenator; supports the nervous system, joints, skin, and reproductive tissues.
- **Yogavahi** — "that which carries": ghee is believed to carry the medicinal qualities of herbs into the deepest tissues — which is why so many Ayurvedic medicines are prepared in a ghee base.

## What Modern Science Confirms

- **High smoke point** — ghee (clarified butter) has a high smoke point (~250°C), so it does not degrade into harmful compounds when cooking — unlike many seed oils.
- **Butyrate** — ghee contains butyric acid, a short-chain fatty acid that supports gut health.
- **Lactose- and casein-free** — because the milk solids are removed in clarification, most lactose-intolerant people tolerate ghee well.
- **Fat-soluble vitamins** — A, D, E, and K, which aid absorption of other nutrients.
- **No trans fats** — when made properly, ghee contains negligible trans fats, unlike partially hydrogenated oils.

## The Heart-Health Reversal

The old "ghee causes heart disease" belief was largely a case of guilt by association — ghee was bundled with a diet that was already high in refined carbs, fried food, and sugar. The nuanced truth:

- **In moderation**, ghee is not the heart problem — **excess sugar, refined flour, trans fats, and deep-fried junk** are.
- The Indian diet's real lipid issue has never been the teaspoon of ghee; it is the biscuits, fried snacks, and sweets.
- A teaspoon or two of quality ghee daily, in a balanced diet, is consistent with a heart-healthy pattern for most people.

## How to Use Ghee: The Wise Way

### Cooking

- **Sautéing and tadka** — the classic use; add to dals, vegetables, and rice.
- **Cooking vegetables** — a teaspoon of ghee with the spices.
- **Golden milk** — a small knob of ghee in warm turmeric milk.
- **Roasting nuts and seeds** — ghee brings out their flavor and nutrients.

### The Daily Rasayana

- **½ to 1 teaspoon** on an empty stomach in the morning, or with warm water, is the classical daily tonic for dry skin, joints, and digestion (for those who digest ghee well).
- **With warm milk at night** — nourishing and sleep-supporting.

### As a Medicine Carrier

- Ayurvedic preparations (*Ghritas*) — medicated ghee cooked with herbs — are a whole class of classical medicines used under physician guidance (e.g., *Shatavari Ghrita*, *Mahatriphala Ghrita*).

## Who Should Be Careful

- **Those with high cholesterol who have genetic lipid disorders or who already consume excess oil** — ghee in moderation (1 teaspoon), not in addition to a high-oil diet.
- **Digestive weakness** — ghee is heavy; those with weak Agni should take it in small amounts, with food, not alone.
- **Obesity** — ghee is calorie-dense; keep the dose to a teaspoon and account for it.
- **Inflammation flares** — in some, excess ghee is heavy; moderate.

The key word is **moderation**: a teaspoon or two daily is the therapeutic range; a deep-fried puri in ghee is not.

## Buying Quality Ghee

- **Grass-fed, traditionally prepared** ghee (from *desi* cows — A2 ghee) is the highest quality.
- Look for **slow-clarified, unadulterated** ghee — no added oils, no preservatives.
- **Color** — golden yellow, not pale (a sign of blending).
- **Aroma** — nutty, buttery, authentic.
- Check the label: 100% pure ghee, no palm oil or vanaspati.

## The Bottom Line

Ghee is not the dietary villain it was made out to be — used correctly, it is a genuine health food and the preferred cooking fat in the Ayurvedic tradition. The wisdom is in **how much** (a teaspoon or two) and **how** (in cooking, with herbs, in golden milk) — and, just as importantly, in what you *don't* eat: the junk, sugar, and fried food that ghee is often unfairly blamed for. Include a little quality ghee, cook with it wisely, and let this "liquid gold" nourish you the way it has for millennia.
