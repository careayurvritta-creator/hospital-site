Yoga is often treated as a workout — but in its classical sense it is a tool for harmonizing the body, breath, and mind, and it is intimately linked with Ayurveda. The two are sister sciences: Ayurveda tells you *what your body needs*, and yoga gives you *the practice to deliver it*. You do not need flexibility, youth, or a boutique studio. You need a small routine, done consistently, matched to your constitution.

## Before You Begin: Three Ground Rules

1. **Start small.** Ten minutes of daily practice beats 60 minutes once a week. Consistency builds the habit; intensity builds injury.
2. **Never force the pose.** Discomfort is fine; sharp pain is a signal to back off. The breath should stay smooth — if you cannot breathe steadily, ease the pose.
3. **Practice on an empty stomach.** Two hours after a meal, or first thing in the morning.

## A Beginner's 15-Minute Routine

### Morning: Awaken and Energize (10 minutes)

1. **Sukhasana (Easy Pose)** — 1 minute. Sit comfortably, eyes closed, 10 slow breaths to center.
2. **Surya Namaskar (Sun Salutations)** — 3–5 rounds. The complete practice in miniature; warms, stretches, and energizes.
3. **Tadasana (Mountain Pose)** — 1 minute. Stand tall, ground the feet, lengthen the spine.
4. **Trikonasana (Triangle Pose)** — 30 seconds each side. Opens the hips and sides.
5. **Bhujangasana (Cobra Pose)** — 30 seconds. Strengthens the back.
6. **Shavasana (Corpse Pose)** — 2 minutes. The essential finale — never skip the rest.

### Evening: Unwind and Ground (5–10 minutes)

1. **Baddha Konasana (Butterfly)** — 1 minute. Opens the hips; calming for the nervous system.
2. **Supta Baddha Konasana** — 2 minutes. Reclined butterfly, supported — deeply restful.
3. **Paschimottanasana (Seated Forward Fold)** — 1 minute. Calms Vata; never force the fold.
4. **Viparita Karani (Legs Up the Wall)** — 3–5 minutes. The single most restorative pose; drains the legs and calms the mind.
5. **Final relaxation with breathing** — 3 minutes of slow belly breathing.

## Matching Yoga to Your Dosha

### Vata constitution (light, flexible, restless)

- **Prefer:** Grounding, steady poses — standing poses, forward folds, long holds, Shavasana.
- **Avoid:** Too much fast movement and over-stretching.

### Pitta constitution (fiery, competitive)

- **Prefer:** Cooling, calming practice — forward folds, seated postures, gentle twists, moon salutations.
- **Avoid:** Hot power yoga in peak heat; practice in the cool morning or evening.

### Kapha constitution (sturdy, strong, sluggish)

- **Prefer:** Dynamic, heating practice — vigorous sun salutations, standing sequences, movement.
- **Avoid:** Long, static, sleepy holds.

## Breath Before Posture

The breath is the bridge between body and mind. A simple rule: **when the mind is scattered, lengthen the breath.** The two most valuable beginner practices:

- **Anulom Vilom** (alternate-nostril breathing) — 5 minutes daily. Balances the nervous system.
- **Bhramari** (humming breath) — 3 rounds before sleep or stress.

## The Ayurvedic Frame Around Yoga

- Practice at the **same time daily** — rhythm amplifies benefit.
- Practice **before breakfast** or in the early evening — never right after a heavy meal.
- Shower or at least wash hands and face before practice.
- End with a few minutes of **silence or stillness** — the integration phase matters as much as the movement.
- On an empty stomach, a glass of warm water before practice aids the process.

## What You'll Notice

In the first week: more ease in the body and a calmer start to the day. By week four: better posture, steadier energy, fewer aches, and a mind that resets faster under stress. In three months, yoga stops being a practice you "do" and becomes a state you carry. Start with the 15-minute routine — the hardest part is the first three weeks. After that, your body will remind you to show up.
