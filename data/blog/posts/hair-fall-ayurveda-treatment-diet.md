Hair is one of the first visible signals of inner health. When hair starts shedding heavily, it is rarely a "hair problem" — it is a body-wide message. In Ayurveda, hair health depends on nourished *Rasa*, *Rakta*, and *Asthi* (bone) tissue, and it is deeply connected to *Pitta* (which governs the hair follicle) and *Vata* (dryness and brittleness). Heavy shedding almost always has an underlying cause that needs identifying first.

## First, Find the Cause

Before any hair care plan makes sense, the root cause should be understood. The most common drivers:

1. **Iron deficiency** — the single most common cause of female hair loss; check ferritin.
2. **Thyroid imbalance** — both low and high thyroid affect hair.
3. **Stress and illness** — telogen effluvium (mass shedding weeks after stress, fever, or childbirth).
4. **Hormonal** — PCOS, menopause, postpartum changes.
5. **Nutritional gaps** — protein, iron, zinc, and B12.
6. **Genetics** — androgenetic hair loss (pattern baldness).

If shedding is sudden, patchy, or accompanied by scalp pain or rash, see a physician for evaluation first.

## Ayurveda's Three-Pronged Hair Plan

### 1. Diet That Feeds the Follicle

- **Protein** at every meal — legumes, dal, paneer, eggs, nuts.
- **Iron-rich foods with vitamin C**: spinach, beetroot, pomegranate, dates + amla.
- **Healthy fats**: ghee, coconut, sesame seeds, almonds.
- **Amla** daily — the classical hair tonic, rich in antioxidants.
- **Biotin-rich foods**: nuts, whole grains, and leafy greens.

### Avoid

- Crash diets and skipping meals.
- Excessive tea, coffee, and caffeine.
- Deep-fried and processed foods.
- Very hot, drying foods and excess spicy intake.

### 2. Scalp and Oil Care (External)

- **Warm sesame oil** or **coconut oil** scalp massage 2–3 times a week — it calms Vata, improves circulation, and strengthens the root. Massage gently for 5–10 minutes.
- **Bhringraj oil** (medicated) — the classical herb for hair; traditional oils combine *Bhringraj*, *Amla*, and *Brahmi*.
- **Onion juice** — used in folk practice and increasingly supported by studies; apply to the scalp, leave 30 minutes, wash. Test on a small area first.
- **Amla rinse** — a final rinse of diluted amla or hibiscus water can condition and reduce hair fall.
- Avoid hot showers, tight hairstyles, and chemical treatments during the healing phase.

### 3. Herbs That Nourish From Within

- **Bhringraj (Eclipta alba)** — the classical hair herb, used internally and externally.
- **Amla** — nourishes the root and supports *Rasayana* (rejuvenation).
- **Ashwagandha** — counters stress-related shedding.
- **Brahmi (Gotu kola)** — supports scalp health and calm.
- **Shatavari** — for hormonal hair loss in women.
- **Aloe vera** — internally (juice) and as a scalp gel; soothes and conditions.

## Managing Stress Shedding

Post-stress shedding (telogen effluvium) is dramatic but usually **self-limiting** — the hair catches up over 3–6 months once the trigger resolves. Support it with:

- 7–8 hours of quality sleep.
- A daily breathing or meditation practice.
- Gentle scalp massage (also stress-relieving).
- Patience — the shed phase is temporary, but the regrowth takes time.

## A Realistic Timeline

Hair grows about 1 cm a month, and the growth cycle runs on a 3-month lag. This means: **whatever you do today will show results on your scalp in 3–6 months.** Improvement in texture and reduced shedding can appear sooner, but visible density changes take a hair cycle. Consistency, not intensity, is what works.
