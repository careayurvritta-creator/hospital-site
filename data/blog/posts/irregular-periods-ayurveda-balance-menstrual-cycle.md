A regular menstrual cycle is one of the most sensitive indicators of a woman's overall health. When periods become irregular — late, scanty, skipped, or painful — it is often the body's way of saying that something deeper is out of balance. In Ayurveda, the menstrual cycle is governed by **Vata** (which moves the blood) and **Kapha** (which forms the tissue), and it is deeply connected to digestion, sleep, stress, and movement.

## The Ayurvedic View of the Cycle

The cycle is understood as a rhythm of the *Artava vaha srotas* (menstrual channels). For the cycle to flow normally:

- **Agni** (digestive fire) must be strong enough to nourish *Rasa* (plasma) and *Rakta* (blood) — the tissues that form the menstrual flow.
- **Vata** must move downward — when it is disturbed, flow becomes irregular or scanty.
- **Ama** must not block the channels — undigested metabolic waste is a classic cause of late and painful periods.

## Common Causes of Irregularity

1. Chronic stress and an overactive mind — cortisol directly suppresses reproductive hormones.
2. Irregular sleep — late nights disturb the hormonal clock.
3. Crash dieting, under-eating, or excessive exercise.
4. PCOS, thyroid disorders, and insulin resistance.
5. Travel and disruption of routine.
6. Suppressing natural urges — including the urge to sleep or rest during menstruation.

## Diet to Support the Cycle

### Through the Month

- Eat warm, cooked, well-spiced food — cold and raw food aggravates Vata.
- Include ghee, sesame seeds, and dates — nourishing for *Rasa* and *Rakta*.
- Add **shatavari** (under guidance) during the follicular phase to support the build-up phase.
- Use iron-rich foods: pomegranate, beetroot, spinach, and jaggery.

### During Menstruation

- Rest more; avoid heavy exercise and long fasting.
- Drink warm fluids: ginger tea, cumin-coriander-fennel water.
- Eat warm, easy meals — khichdi, soups, cooked vegetables.
- Avoid cold drinks, fried food, and vigorous work.

## Herbs Commonly Used

- **Shatavari** — the classical female tonic; nourishes reproductive tissue and supports hormonal balance.
- **Ashokarishta** — a classical formulation for gynecological care, traditionally used for irregular and heavy periods.
- **Aloe vera** — taken internally (pulp or juice) in small amounts, traditionally used for the menstrual cycle; use cautiously, as it is potent.
- **Rajapravartini vati** — a classical formula used under supervision for delayed periods.
- **Dashamoola** — calms Vata and is used in supportive combinations.

**Important:** Emmenagogues (period-inducing herbs) must never be used casually — especially if pregnancy is possible. Always confirm the cause with a physician.

## The Role of Panchakarma

For recurring irregularity, a supervised course of **Basti** — especially *Uttara Basti* (medicated enema via the reproductive tract, used by trained physicians) — steadies Vata at its root and is classical for menstrual disorders. **Virechana** clears Ama and Pitta-related congestion. These are always performed after preparation and under medical supervision.

## Daily Practices That Restore Rhythm

1. Sleep by 10:30–11 PM — the hormonal clock runs on sleep.
2. Move daily, but avoid over-exercising — balance, not extremes.
3. Practice **yoga for the pelvis**: Baddha Konasana, Supta Baddha Konasana, and gentle twists.
4. Do 10 minutes of **Anulom Vilom** (alternate-nostril breathing) daily — it steadies Vata and calms the mind.
5. Eat at regular times; never skip meals.

## When to Seek Help

If your periods are irregular for more than two to three cycles, if you have very heavy bleeding, if you are trying to conceive, or if there is pelvic pain and bleeding between periods, please see a physician. Ayurvedic care is powerful — but it must work alongside proper evaluation, including a pelvic examination, hormonal profile, and ultrasound when needed.
