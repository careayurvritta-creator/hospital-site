Urticaria — hives — appears suddenly as itchy, raised, red welts (wheals) that can change shape and location within hours. It is one of the most common skin complaints, and in Ayurveda it is classically known as **Sheetapitta** or *Udarda*: a disturbance of **Pitta** (heat, redness) and **Kapha** (swelling, wheals), with Vata's role in their sudden, wandering movement. The welts are the skin's way of saying the internal balance has been disturbed — and the approach is to cool the fire and calm the reaction, not just suppress the itch.

## Why Hives Happen

1. **Food reactions** — the most common trigger in acute cases: shellfish, eggs, peanuts, milk, certain fruits, and food additives.
2. **Drug reactions** — including common ones like aspirin and antibiotics.
3. **Infections** — viral illnesses often precede an outbreak.
4. **Physical triggers** — heat, cold, pressure, sunlight, water, sweating.
5. **Stress** — a well-documented trigger and amplifier.
6. **Underlying conditions** — thyroid autoimmunity and other chronic issues are linked to chronic urticaria.

## The Two Types

### Acute urticaria (less than 6 weeks)

- Usually a clear trigger — a food, drug, or infection.
- Resolves with removal of the trigger.

### Chronic urticaria (more than 6 weeks)

- Often no clear trigger; may be autoimmune.
- Needs a physician's evaluation; treatment is about reducing reactivity, not finding one "cause."

## The Ayurvedic Plan for Calmer Skin

### 1. Cooling the Pitta

- **Cooling foods:** cucumber, coconut, melon, leafy greens, bottle gourd.
- **Reduce:** spicy, fried, fermented, and sour foods; excess oil.
- **Sweet, bitter, and astringent tastes** pacify Pitta — favor them.
- **Cool compresses** on the welts for immediate comfort.
- Avoid hot showers, hot rooms, and tight clothing.

### 2. Clearing the Ama

Hives often follow a period of poor digestion — the skin is the body's second elimination organ:

- A few days of **light, warm, easy-to-digest food** (khichdi, soups).
- **Triphala** at night for regular elimination.
- **Warm water** through the day.
- Reduce sugar and processed food.

### 3. Calming the Reaction

- **Manjistha** — the classical blood-purifying herb for skin conditions.
- **Neem** — cooling and blood-cleaning; internal and external use.
- **Guduchi (Giloy)** — immune-modulating; valuable for allergic skin conditions.
- **Turmeric** — anti-inflammatory.
- **Tulsi** — a mild natural antihistamine; tulsi tea or fresh leaves.
- **Amla** — antioxidant support.

## External Comfort During a Flare

- **Cool compress** or cool (not cold) bath with a little oatmeal — soothes the itch.
- **Neem water wash** — boiled, cooled neem water as a rinse.
- **Coconut oil or plain aloe gel** — gentle moisturizing (test on a small patch first).
- **Avoid** harsh soaps, fragrances, and scratching (scratching worsens the cycle).

## The Diet Diary: Finding Your Trigger

For recurrent hives, a **symptom diary** is the most powerful tool:

- Record: what you ate, medicines taken, stress level, and when the welts appeared.
- Within 2–4 weeks, patterns become visible.
- Common repeat offenders to watch: **shellfish, peanuts, milk, eggs, citrus, tomatoes, food colorings, and preservatives.**

If you suspect a specific food, remove it for 2 weeks, then reintroduce carefully and note the response — but for serious reactions, do this only with medical supervision.

## Stress and the Immune Skin

Chronic urticaria is strongly linked to stress and an overactive immune system:

- A daily **breathing or meditation practice** (10 minutes).
- Adequate sleep — the immune skin calms at night.
- Gentle exercise — walk, swim, yoga; avoid overheating.
- Realistic pacing — stress management is not optional for chronic hives.

## When You Need a Doctor

Please seek care if hives are accompanied by:

- **Swelling of the lips, tongue, or throat** — a possible emergency (angioedema).
- **Difficulty breathing or wheezing**.
- **Dizziness or fainting**.
- **Fever**.
- Welts that persist beyond **6 weeks** — chronic urticaria deserves proper evaluation.

For chronic cases, modern management (antihistamines under guidance) works well alongside Ayurvedic care — the two are complementary, not competing.

## A Realistic Outlook

Acute hives usually settle within days to weeks once the trigger is removed. Chronic urticaria is more a tendency than a one-time event — the goal is **fewer, milder, shorter episodes**, achieved by cooling the system, identifying triggers, and calming the stress response. Most patients notice meaningful improvement within 4–8 weeks of a consistent plan, and many find that over time their skin reactivity genuinely calms.
