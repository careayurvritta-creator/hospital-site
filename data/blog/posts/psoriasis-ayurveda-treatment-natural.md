Psoriasis is a chronic skin condition in which the skin's renewal cycle speeds up dramatically, producing thick, silvery scales and red, inflamed patches. Modern medicine treats it as an immune-mediated condition — and Ayurveda agrees, though in a different language. It falls under the classical umbrella of **Kushtha**, a family of skin disorders understood as arising from deeply disturbed doshas, weakened digestion, and accumulated *Ama* (metabolic toxins) that surface through the skin.

## The Ayurvedic Mechanism

In psoriasis, the doshas that dominate are **Vata** (dryness, scaling, roughness) and **Kapha** (thickness, itching, exudation), often with **Pitta** involvement in the redness and burning. The root, however, is usually *Ama* — the skin is the body's largest eliminating organ, and when the deeper channels are clogged, waste surfaces there.

This is why topical creams alone never cure psoriasis: the trigger is systemic, and the treatment must be systemic too.

## Diet: Calming the Inner Fire

### Strongly Avoid

- Sour foods: curd, pickles, vinegar, fermented foods.
- Salty and spicy foods that aggravate Pitta and Vata.
- Non-vegetarian food, especially red meat and seafood — classical texts advise against these in skin disease.
- Fried, processed, and reheated food.
- Alcohol and excessive tea or coffee.

### Prefer

- Warm, fresh, lightly cooked meals — khichdi, soups, steamed vegetables.
- Bitter vegetables: bitter gourd, fenugreek, neem leaves (in small amounts).
- Barley, millets, and old rice.
- Warm water through the day.
- Fresh pomegranate, amla, and sweet ripe fruits in moderation.

## The Herb Arsenal (Under Guidance)

- **Neem (Nimba)** — the classic blood purifier; used internally and externally.
- **Manjistha** — the classical herb for skin, blood, and lymph; gives skin its health.
- **Khadira (Acacia catechu)** — a specific for skin disease in classical texts; *Khadirarishta* is a traditional formulation.
- **Guduchi (Giloy)** — immune-modulating support.
- **Turmeric** — anti-inflammatory; combines well with neem.
- **Sarnabeej / Bitter formulations** — classical compounds like *Kaishore Guggulu* and *Arogyavardhini Vati* are used under supervision.

## Topical Ayurvedic Care

Gentle, medicated external applications can reduce scaling and itching:

- **Neem and turmeric paste** applied to patches.
- **Manjistha decoction** used as a wash.
- Medicated oils — traditionally, *Pancha Nimba Taila* and *Khadira* preparations are used.
- Avoid harsh scrubbing; moisturize with plain coconut oil.

**Note:** Skin in psoriasis is fragile. Even herbal applications should be started gently and under guidance — some people react to ingredients like garlic or lemon juice.

## The Panchakarma Approach

The classical treatment for stubborn skin disease is a course of **Vamana** (therapeutic emesis) and/or **Virechana** (therapeutic purgation) — designed to clear Ama and Pitta from the system, followed by *Rasayana* (rejuvenation) therapy to rebuild healthy tissue. Combined with medicated oil massage and steam, this is the deep treatment that addresses the root. It must always be performed by a qualified physician.

## The Stress Connection

Psoriasis flares dramatically with stress — it is one of the best-documented trigger-symptom links in dermatology. Alongside diet and herbs:

- A daily 10–15 minute breathing practice (Anulom Vilom).
- Gentle, regular yoga.
- Sleep by 10:30 PM.
- Kindness to yourself — the psychological weight of a visible skin condition is real, and managing it is part of treatment.

## A Realistic Outlook

Psoriasis is a long-term condition. Ayurvedic treatment aims not for a "magic cure" but for **longer remissions, thinner patches, and less itch** — achieved by correcting the terrain. Improvement typically begins in 6–12 weeks of consistent care, with deeper courses (Panchakarma) giving the most durable results. Throughout, your dermatologist and Ayurvedic physician can work together — many patients do best on a combined approach.
