Eczema is the itch that turns into a rash — and the rash that turns into a habit of scratching. It affects children and adults alike, and it has a remarkably consistent story in Ayurveda: a **Pitta–Kapha disturbance** with deep *Ama* involvement. The skin is inflamed (Pitta), oozing or thick (Kapha), and sensitive (a sensitized immune response we call disturbed *Rakta* and *Rasa dhatu*).

## Understanding Your Skin Type

### Pitta-dominant eczema

- Red, hot, burning patches.
- Itching worse at night and in heat.
- Aggravated by spicy, sour, or fermented food.

### Kapha-dominant eczema

- Thick, oozing, weeping patches.
- Worse in damp, cold weather.
- Associated with slow digestion and a feeling of heaviness.

### Vata-dominant (dry) eczema

- Rough, cracked, flaky skin.
- Worse in dry, windy weather and winter.
- Aggravated by cold, dry food and dehydration.

## Diet: What to Eat and What to Avoid

### Avoid

- Sour and fermented foods: curd, pickles, vinegar, soy sauce.
- Spicy, fried, and processed foods.
- Excess tea, coffee, and alcohol.
- Nightshades in sensitive individuals — tomatoes, potatoes, brinjal (test individually).
- Excess citrus and very cold foods.

### Prefer

- Warm, simple, cooked meals.
- Bitter and cooling foods: bottle gourd, cucumber, leafy greens.
- Ghee and coconut — healthy fats that nourish the skin barrier.
- Amla, pomegranate, and sweet ripe fruits.
- Warm water and mild herbal teas.

## Herbs That Calm the Skin

- **Manjistha** — the classical blood-and-skin herb; taken internally for a healthy complexion.
- **Neem** — cools, purifies, and supports the skin's immune response.
- **Guduchi (Giloy)** — modulates the immune system; valuable in allergic skin conditions.
- **Shatavari** — especially for Pitta-dominant eczema and dry skin.
- **Turmeric** — anti-inflammatory and antioxidant; combine with ghee and milk for a traditional drink.
- **Trikatu and Triphala** — improve digestion, reducing the Ama that surfaces on the skin.

## External Care That Helps

- **Coconut oil** or plain ghee — gentle moisturizing for dry, cracked skin.
- **Neem water wash** — boil neem leaves, cool, and use as a soothing rinse.
- **Oatmeal baths** — an old and effective remedy for itching (this is a safe modern addition).
- **Cool compresses** — for hot, burning patches.
- Avoid harsh soaps; use mild, fragrance-free cleansers.

## The Role of Ama and Digestion

A weak digestive fire produces *Ama*, which accumulates and surfaces through the skin. So the "skin treatment" in Ayurveda is often really a **digestive treatment**:

1. Eat your largest meal at noon.
2. Keep dinner light and early.
3. Take a pinch of ginger and rock salt with meals to kindle Agni.
4. A teaspoon of Triphala at night supports regularity.

## Panchakarma for Stubborn Eczema

Chronic eczema is classically treated with **Virechana** (therapeutic purgation) to clear Pitta and Ama, followed by blood-purifying (*Rakta prasadana*) and rejuvenating (*Rasayana*) therapy. This is a supervised, in-clinic or physician-guided course — not something to attempt at home.

## A Gentle, Realistic Path

Eczema is chronic and can flare unpredictably. The goal of Ayurvedic care is **fewer, shorter, milder flares** and healthier skin between them. Many patients notice calmer skin within 4–8 weeks of consistent diet and herb care. And importantly: if eczema is severe, weeping, or infected, involve both your dermatologist and Ayurvedic physician — severe flares sometimes need modern management alongside the healing plan.
