Sleep is not a luxury — it is the time when the body repairs, the brain consolidates memory, and the nervous system resets. When sleep is broken, every other system suffers. In Ayurveda, sound sleep (*Nidra*) is one of the three pillars of health, alongside food (*Ahara*) and disciplined conduct (*Brahmacharya*). Insomnia is understood as a disturbed state of **Vata** (racing mind, light sleep) or **Pitta** (waking hot and irritable at 2–3 AM), and it is almost always aggravated by the modern habits of late nights and blue light.

## Why You Can't Sleep: The Common Causes

1. Irregular sleep-wake times — the body clock drifts.
2. Stimulation at night — screens, work email, intense conversation.
3. Heavy or late dinner — undigested food disturbs sleep.
4. Excess caffeine, especially after noon.
5. Daytime inactivity — an underused body cannot switch off.
6. Worrying in bed — the bed becomes associated with thinking.
7. Blood sugar dips at night — waking at 3–4 AM is often this.

## The Ayurvedic Sleep Routine

### The Hour Before Bed

1. **Dim the lights** 60–90 minutes before sleep. Blue light suppresses melatonin — the master sleep hormone.
2. **No screens in bed.** Charge the phone in another room if needed.
3. **Warm milk** with a pinch of nutmeg, turmeric, and a little ghee — the classical Ayurvedic nightcap.
4. **Foot massage** with warm oil for 5 minutes — calming and grounding.
5. **Cool the room** — Ayurveda recommends a cool, dark, quiet bedroom.
6. **A short walk or gentle stretching** after dinner aids digestion.

### The Daytime Preparation

- Wake at a **fixed early time**, even on weekends.
- Get **morning sunlight** for 15 minutes — it anchors the body clock.
- Move daily — physical tiredness is the best natural sedative.
- Eat dinner **by 7–8 PM**, light and warm.
- Stop caffeine **after 2 PM**.

## Herbs That Support Sleep

- **Ashwagandha** — supports the stress-sleep axis; calming for the racing Vata mind.
- **Jatamansi** — a classical herb for restlessness and disturbed sleep.
- **Brahmi** — for the overthinking mind.
- **Sarpagandha** — a classical herb for the agitated mind; potent, used under supervision, never casually.
- **Tagara (Valerian)** — traditionally used for sleep; gentle in proper doses.
- **Nutmeg** — the time-honored spice; a tiny pinch in warm milk. Excess nutmeg is toxic, so always keep doses minimal.

**Important:** Never combine these herbs with sedative sleeping pills without your physician's knowledge — interactions can be dangerous.

## When Waking at 3 AM Is a Blood Sugar Issue

Waking at the same time each night, often 2–4 AM, with a racing or hungry feeling, frequently points to a **nighttime blood sugar dip** (the "dawn phenomenon" opposite — reactive hypoglycemia). The fix is dietary:

- A light, protein-containing dinner — not a heavy carb load.
- A small pre-bed snack if needed: warm milk, a few almonds, or a banana.
- No sugar in the evening.
- Regular daytime meals to stabilize the 24-hour blood sugar rhythm.

## Panchakarma and Deep Sleep

For chronic, stubborn insomnia rooted in long-standing Vata disturbance, a course of **Shirodhara** — the slow, rhythmic pouring of warm medicated oil on the forehead — is the classical treatment. Shirodhara is profoundly calming for the nervous system, and many patients report the deepest sleep they've had in years during a course. It is performed in a clinic, under supervision.

## A Realistic Approach

Sleep habits take 2–4 weeks to reset. Start with the basics — fixed wake time, no screens in bed, warm milk — before reaching for herbs, and use herbs before considering stronger measures. If insomnia persists beyond a month, or is accompanied by depression, snoring, or daytime exhaustion, consult a physician — there may be sleep apnea or another cause worth treating properly.
