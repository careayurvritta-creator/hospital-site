Ashwagandha (*Withania somnifera*) has become the face of Ayurveda in the global wellness world — and for good reason: it is one of the most studied adaptogenic herbs, with genuine evidence behind its use for stress, sleep, and stamina. But its popularity has also created a wave of casual, self-prescribed, often incorrect use. This guide gives you the evidence, the proper dose, and — just as important — the honest warnings. Ashwagandha is medicine, and it should be used like one.

## What the Evidence Actually Shows

The most consistently supported benefits:

- **Stress reduction** — multiple studies show lower cortisol and reduced perceived stress with consistent use.
- **Anxiety and mood** — modest improvements in anxiety scores, particularly in chronically stressed people.
- **Sleep** — improved sleep quality and latency in several trials; it is genuinely useful for sleep-onset difficulty.
- **Strength and stamina** — modest improvements in muscle strength and recovery in exercising adults.
- **Male health** — some evidence for sperm quality and testosterone in specific populations.
- **Mild thyroid support** — some studies show improved thyroid markers in subclinical hypothyroidism (use with caution — see warnings).

## How It Works (The Ayurvedic Frame)

- **Rasayana** — a rejuvenator that nourishes the deepest tissues.
- **Vata–Kapha pacifying** — grounding, warming, strengthening.
- The root, not the leaf, is the classical part used.

## Dosing: The Honest Numbers

- **Standard range:** 300–600 mg of **standardized extract** (root/root extract, ~2.5–5% withanolides) once or twice daily.
- **Powder (whole root):** 3–6 grams daily, often taken with warm milk and ghee — the classical preparation.
- **Best taken:** with food (it is fat-soluble — warm milk or a meal with fat improves absorption), typically in the evening for sleep or morning for energy, depending on the goal.
- **Duration:** cycles of 8–12 weeks, with breaks, are the common clinical pattern; continuous daily use for years is not the traditional model.

**The key point:** dose depends on the preparation (extract vs. powder are not interchangeable). Follow the label of a quality brand, or better, a physician's guidance.

## Who Should Be Careful (Warnings)

This is where the internet gets it wrong. Ashwagandha is not for everyone:

- **Pregnancy** — avoid; it has traditionally been used (with other herbs) as an abortifacient in large doses. Do not use in pregnancy.
- **Hyperthyroidism** — it can raise thyroid activity; use only under physician guidance in hyperthyroid states.
- **Autoimmune disease** (RA, lupus, MS, Hashimoto's with active flare) — it stimulates immune activity; controversial and should be physician-managed.
- **On sedatives, thyroid medication, or immunosuppressants** — can interact; always inform your doctor.
- **Peptic ulcers and gastritis** — can aggravate in some people; use with care.
- **Low blood pressure** — it can lower blood pressure further.
- **Surgery** — stop a few weeks before planned surgery (effects on anesthesia and blood sugar).

## Side Effects to Know

Most people tolerate it well, but possible side effects include:

- **Stomach upset, nausea, or diarrhea** — usually dose-related; reduce the dose or take with food.
- **Drowsiness** — avoid driving at peak doses until you know your response.
- **Headache or dizziness.**
- **Skin rash** (rare).
- **Rare liver reports** — very uncommon, but those with existing liver disease should be cautious. Stop and consult if you notice jaundice, dark urine, or unexplained fatigue.

## Quality and Buying

The market is full of substandard products. Buy:

- **Reputable brands** with third-party testing (USP, NSF, or similar).
- **Standardized extracts** — you know the withanolide content.
- **Root-based products** — the classical part.
- Avoid unlabeled powders of unknown origin.

## The Bottom Line

Ashwagandha is a genuinely valuable herb — for the right person, at the right dose, for the right duration. It is not a daily candy to be taken year-round by everyone. Used correctly — on a cycle, at a proper dose, with attention to warnings — it is a remarkable Rasayana. Used casually, it can cause more harm than benefit. When in doubt, consult an Ayurvedic physician who can match the herb, dose, and timing to your specific constitution and health picture.
