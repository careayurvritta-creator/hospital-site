Gastritis — inflammation of the stomach lining — is one of the most common digestive complaints, and one that affects the whole person: the gnawing pain, the fullness, the burning, the way stress makes everything worse. In Ayurveda, gastritis is understood as a **Pitta disturbance of the stomach** (*Amla Pitta* with *Urdhwaga* tendencies). Pitta is the fire that digests food; when it becomes excessive and hot, it burns the very lining it should nourish. The Ayurvedic answer is to **cool and soothe the lining** while removing the fire-feeding habits.

## The Common Triggers

1. **Irregular eating** — long gaps, skipped meals, then heavy eating.
2. **Spicy, fried, and oily food** in excess.
3. **Excess tea, coffee, and alcohol** — especially on an empty stomach.
4. **Chronic stress** — the single biggest amplifier.
5. **Painkillers (NSAIDs)** — regular aspirin or ibuprofen is a common cause.
6. **H. pylori infection** — a treatable bacterial cause worth testing for in persistent cases.
7. **Smoking.**

## The Immediate Soothe

### Home remedies that actually help

- **Cold milk** with a teaspoon of ghee — coats and cools the lining (the classic evening remedy).
- **Amla** — fresh amla juice or powder in warm water, in the morning; a natural Pitta coolant.
- **Shatavari** — especially for women; soothing to the stomach lining.
- **Licorice (Mulethi)** — a small piece chewed after meals soothes the lining (use sparingly; not for high blood pressure or prolonged use).
- **Coriander and fennel water** — a teaspoon of seeds boiled in water, sipped warm; gentle and cooling.
- **Coconut water** — cool and soothing.
- **Cucumber and bottle gourd** — cooling foods.

## The Gastritis Diet

### Prefer (cooling, soothing)

- Warm, light, freshly cooked meals — khichdi, soups, rice with moong dal.
- Sweet, ripe fruits — banana, melon, pomegranate.
- Cooked vegetables — bottle gourd, ridge gourd, zucchini.
- Ghee, milk, and fresh yogurt (not sour).
- Coconut and tender coconut water.
- Small, frequent meals rather than large ones.

### Avoid (fire-feeding)

- Red chilies, excessive garlic and onion, pickles.
- Fried, fermented, and very sour foods.
- Tea, coffee, alcohol, and carbonated drinks — especially empty stomach.
- Citrus fruits in excess (lemon is fine in small amounts with water, but not in excess during a flare).
- Late, heavy dinners and lying down right after meals.
- Painkillers on an empty stomach (take them with food when possible, and minimize use).

## Stress: The Hidden Flame

Gastritis and stress are intimately linked — the stomach is a barometer of the nervous system:

1. **A daily breathing practice** — 10 minutes of slow breathing or Anulom Vilom.
2. **Eat slowly and mindfully** — the body digests best when calm.
3. **A 5–10 minute walk after meals** — aids digestion and calms the nervous system.
4. **Adequate sleep** — the stomach repairs at night.
5. **Don't eat in a hurry or while stressed** — this is when gastritis flares.

## The Role of Virechana

For chronic, recurrent gastritis, a physician-guided **Virechana** (therapeutic purgation) — the classical Pitta cleanse — can clear the excess heat and Ama from the digestive tract. It is performed after preparation, under supervision, followed by a specific recovery diet. This is the deep treatment for a chronically inflamed stomach.

## When to See a Doctor

Persistent gastritis deserves proper evaluation. Please see a physician if you have:

- **Pain that persists** beyond 2–3 weeks despite care.
- **Blood** in the vomit or stool (black, tarry stool is a red flag).
- **Unexplained weight loss**.
- **Difficulty swallowing**.
- **Frequent vomiting**.
- A **family history of stomach cancer** — earlier evaluation is wise.

An **H. pylori test** (breath, stool, or scope) is worth doing for persistent symptoms — it is a treatable cause that, if left, can lead to ulcers. Testing is simple, and treatment is effective.

## A Realistic Path

Most gastritis settles within **2–4 weeks** of consistent cooling diet, stress management, and regular meals. For chronic cases, the combination of modern investigation (H. pylori testing, acid control as needed) and Ayurvedic cooling care is often the fastest route to a calm stomach. The lasting fix is not the remedy — it is the rhythm: regular meals, cooling food, and a calmer mind. Protect the lining daily, and it will protect you.
